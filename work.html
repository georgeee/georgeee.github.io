Molt Petit · Yak Software

A research paper, in draft. A consensus protocol built to be small: a fixed roster, a round-robin slot schedule and one density rule in place of votes, quorum certificates and view change. A non-specialist can follow it in a minute; it finalises deterministically and fast, and a light client needs to hold almost nothing.

The depth is in the threat analysis. Rotating a key, having a key extracted, and someone holding a key for a while are three separate events with different consequences, and each has a defined path back to safety. The light-client safety results are proved in Lean 4 — about fifteen thousand lines — against the Rust implementation.

Much of the proof search and iteration was done with AI agents, which became part of the research question rather than merely a productivity tool. Preprint and artifact.

Tezos tooling in Haskell · Serokell

I led several blockchain projects in Haskell and OCaml.

Among them were Morley, a typed Haskell embedding of Tezos's Michelson, and Indigo, a more conventional language built on top of it. Much of the interesting work was in making Michelson's stack typing explicit enough that invalid programs could be rejected before they reached the chain.
